Internal duplication and a possible CMD-family origin of DehI

A structure-guided profile-HMM analysis of active-site enclosure and enzyme-fold evolution

Author

Jason Schmidberger

Published

September 8, 2026

Summary

The configuration-inverting 2-haloacid dehalogenase DehI has two structurally similar regions of approximately 130 amino acids. This analysis tests whether the two regions retain detectable sequence homology consistent with an ancient tandem duplication and fusion.

The N- and C-terminal regions were defined using the experimentally determined DehI structure 3BJX, extracted from a 69-sequence family alignment, converted into separate HH-suite profile hidden Markov models, and compared using HHalign. The profiles align over nearly their entire lengths with 99.54% HHalign probability, an E-value of 2.9 × 10-23, and 17% direct sequence identity. The combination of low identity, strong profile-profile support, and broad alignment coverage is consistent with remote homology between the two DehI repeats.

Broad CMD-like and AhpD profiles produced no statistically convincing match to either DehI half. The compact CMD proteins were therefore divided into sequence-coherent monomer subfamilies before profile construction. This revealed reproducible but sub-threshold signals: the strongest were DehI-N/CMD_monomer_017 at 87.72% probability and DehI-N/CMD_monomer_015 at 85.88%. CMD_monomer_015 also matched DehI-C at 80.99% over 64 columns. The first three corrected P-values remain below 0.001 after a conservative correction across 54 comparisons, but none reaches the usual ≥95% HH-suite probability expected for a confident assignment.

If this relationship is confirmed, its importance extends beyond family assignment. Compact CMD-family proteins present their functional surface to bulk solvent, whereas DehI performs direct hydrolytic dehalogenation in a buried active-site cavity formed within its duplicated architecture. Tandem duplication and fusion could therefore have internalised an ancestral exposed surface, creating a new physicochemical environment and enabling fundamentally different catalytic chemistry. This is the central evolutionary hypothesis of the report; the present sequence analysis makes it plausible but does not yet establish the ancestry, direction, or historical order of those changes.

CMD/AhpD structures provide a relevant but deliberately separate precedent. The compact CMD-family protein TTHA0727 forms a six-core homohexamer, whereas trimeric AhpD packages six related cores as two core-like units within each chain. This correspondence shows that duplication and fusion have redistributed CMD-like cores between quaternary and tertiary structure in this broader group. It does not make AhpD an ancestor of, or an intermediate toward, DehI.

NoteWhy the possible CMD relationship matters

The proposed transition is not simply a change in substrate specificity. It would couple internal duplication and fusion to active-site enclosure: an ancestrally solvent-exposed CMD-like surface would become part of a buried, asymmetric reaction chamber in DehI. That change could alter water organisation, electrostatics, residue pKa values, substrate orientation, and halide-leaving group stabilisation simultaneously.

Biological question

DehI from Pseudomonas putida PP3 is an unusual α-helical dehalogenase with a deep Stevedore knot. Its monomer contains two regions with closely similar structures but low pairwise sequence identity. The original structural study described two approximately 130-residue domains with about 16% sequence identity (PDB 3BJX; DOI: 10.1016/j.jmb.2008.02.035). A later topology analysis interpreted these regions as likely products of tandem sequence duplication and described the proline-rich arc connecting them (King et al., 2010).

The specific question addressed here is:

Does a profile-profile comparison of the DehI N- and C-terminal families recover statistically strong, domain-scale sequence homology after the linker is excluded?

A second, exploratory question asks whether either DehI repeat retains a detectable relationship to intact, compact carboxymuconolactone decarboxylase (CMD)-like proteins or alkyl hydroperoxide reductase D (AhpD) proteins.

Evolutionary hypothesis: from exposed surface to buried active site

Structural precedent for duplication and fusion

The oligomeric architectures of compact CMD-family proteins and AhpD provide precedent for duplication and fusion within the broader structural group. The compact CMD-family member TTHA0727 (PDB 2CWQ) forms a D3-symmetric homohexamer built from six single-core polypeptide chains. By contrast, Mycobacterium tuberculosis AhpD (PDB 1KNC) is a homotrimer whose subunit contains two structurally related core-like units. Structural comparison shows that the AhpD trimer and compact CMD hexamer therefore contain an equivalent six-core arrangement, with true two-fold relationships between separate CMD chains replaced by intrachain pseudo-dyads in AhpD (Ito et al., 2006; Andreeva et al., 2007).

This is evidence for an accessible type of evolutionary operation: related CMD-like cores can be duplicated and fused, transferring structural repetition from oligomeric assembly into a single polypeptide. It is not evidence that AhpD lies on a CMD-to-DehI trajectory, nor that any particular oligomer was a stable intermediate in DehI evolution. The DehI proposal below is an independent duplication-and-fusion hypothesis whose distinctive consequence would be active-site enclosure and formation of the knotted architecture.

The structural transition

The possible CMD-family connection is biologically consequential because the same local fold would operate in two very different physical settings. Compact CMD-family proteins are small all-α units in which the relevant binding or catalytic region remains accessible from bulk solvent. The structure of TTHA0727 identified a solvent-accessible cleft as its putative small-molecule binding site (Ito et al., 2006). In a structurally related AhpD-like protein, the catalytic cysteines and adjacent surface cavity remain externally accessible in both the monomer and oligomer (Nunn et al., 2011).

DehI presents the opposite biochemical context. Its two internally homologous regions pack into a pseudo-dimer surrounding a buried active-site cavity. The 3BJX structure places the proposed water-activating Asp189 in this enclosed site, supporting direct nucleophilic attack on either substrate enantiomer (Schmidberger et al., 2008).

In this model, each duplicated unit need not adopt an entirely unrelated local fold. The innovation occurs when fusion and subsequent packing change the context of the conserved fold. A previously exposed surface becomes an interdomain interface and reaction chamber, potentially changing several catalytically important properties at once:

  • solvent and water organisation around the reacting centre;
  • local electrostatics and catalytic-residue pKa values;
  • substrate orientation and residence time;
  • exclusion of non-productive bulk water; and
  • stabilisation and release of the halide leaving group.

The two copies could then diverge asymmetrically. The C-terminal repeat could specialise in water activation and catalytic chemistry, while the N-terminal repeat increasingly contributes enclosure, substrate positioning, access control, and structural support. This would convert duplicated structural symmetry into a functionally asymmetric enzyme.

Evolutionary importance and fitness

This would be an unusually substantial evolutionary transition: duplication and fusion would connect a new overall topology, active-site burial, and new chemistry rather than merely retuning an existing exposed pocket. The CMD/AhpD oligomeric comparison establishes that duplication and fusion are structurally precedented in the group, but it does not identify a viable intermediate or show how dehalogenase activity emerged. Those questions remain independent of the architectural precedent.

An immediate selective advantage is plausible but cannot be reconstructed from the present sequences alone. Initial retention might also have been assisted by ancestral catalytic promiscuity, improved stability or assembly, increased dosage, or a period of effectively neutral persistence. Experimental studies of other enzyme families show that weak ancestral side activities can become the principal functions of post-duplication descendants (Voordeckers et al., 2012).

The ecological context supports selection for the mature activity without dating the original innovation. PP3 was isolated under growth selection on 2,2-dichloropropionic acid, and its dehI family gene lies on the mobile DEH element with its regulator, flanked by insertion sequences (Webster et al., 2025). Horizontal transfer could therefore have delivered an already functional DehI enzyme to PP3, where haloacid utilisation supplied an immediate advantage, even if the deeper duplication-and-fusion event occurred elsewhere.

Evidence boundaries

Analysis design

The workflow deliberately separates computation from presentation. Alignment splitting and HH-suite profile construction are performed by version-controlled scripts. This document reads the resulting files; it does not rerun HH-suite during website rendering.

Defining and extracting the repeats

The last long helix of the N-terminal repeat ends at 3BJX residue 129. A proline-rich region occupies residues 130–161, and the next repeat begins at residue 162 with a short 310 helix followed by the first long C-terminal helix. The linker is therefore 32 residues by these structure-defined bounds, corresponding to the approximately 30-residue linker described informally.

The boundaries were mapped from native 3BJX residue numbers to MSA columns. The same columns were then extracted from every family member. All-gap columns were removed independently from each resulting alignment. The procedure is implemented in split_dehi_msa.py.

The N-terminal output has 139 aligned columns and the C-terminal output has 167. The ungapped 3BJX fragments contain 129 and 135 residues, respectively. All 69 input records are represented in both outputs.

HH-suite profile construction

HH-suite was chosen because HHalign directly compares profile HMMs and is designed to detect remote protein homology through alignment-to-alignment or HMM-to-HMM comparison (HH-suite documentation; Steinegger et al., 2019).

Each split FASTA alignment was converted to A3M format. A column was designated as a match state when fewer than 50% of sequences contained a gap (-M 50). Columns not meeting this condition were represented as insert states. HHmake then applied its standard sequence filtering and weighting before constructing each HHM.

reformat.pl fas a3m results/dehi_N_terminal_msa.fasta \
  results/dehi_N_terminal.a3m -M 50
reformat.pl fas a3m results/dehi_C_terminal_msa.fasta \
  results/dehi_C_terminal.a3m -M 50

hhmake -i results/dehi_N_terminal.a3m \
  -o results/dehi_N_terminal.hhm -name DehI_N_terminal
hhmake -i results/dehi_C_terminal.a3m \
  -o results/dehi_C_terminal.hhm -name DehI_C_terminal

HHmake retained 68 of 69 sequences in each model after its default redundancy and quality filters. The effective sequence counts (Neff) of 5.5 and 6.0 are much smaller than the raw sequence count because related sequences do not contribute as independent observations.

N-terminal versus C-terminal HHalign result

The two precomputed HHMs were compared directly:

hhalign \
  -i results/dehi_N_terminal.hhm \

The alignment spans N-profile match states 5–113 and C-profile match states 2–112. It therefore covers most of both models rather than selecting a small highly conserved segment. The 17% direct identity lies in the range where pairwise sequence comparison alone is generally difficult, while the profile score remains strong because residue preferences and gap patterns are aggregated across the family.

Comparison with CMD-like and AhpD proteins

Defining the reference families

The external comparisons use two UniProt-derived, 60%-identity-reduced source sets already present in the project. These labels need care. Pfam PF02627 is named for CMD but represents the broader AhpD/CMD-like fold; bona fide AhpD proteins can also carry PF02627. The two groups below are therefore operational sequence sets, not mutually exclusive fold classifications. This relationship is independently reflected in the domain annotation of an AhpD example (UniProt P9WQB4), the HAMAP AhpD rule, and the 2OUW structural annotations.

  • CMD-like: PF02627 records explicitly annotated as carboxymuconolactone decarboxylase, excluding descriptions containing AhpD, and restricted to 80–220 residues to reduce fragments and fusions.
  • AhpD: records from the more specific IPR004674 source set, restricted to 120–230 residues.

Two non-standard residues in the CMD-like set were converted to X; none were converted in the AhpD set. Selection and normalization are implemented in prepare_cmd_ahpd_comparison.py. The retained sequences were aligned independently with MAFFT FFT-NS-1, converted to A3M with the same -M 50 match-state rule as the DehI halves, and passed to HHmake. The complete pipeline is in build_cmd_ahpd_hhsuite_profiles.sh.

Monomer-focused CMD analysis

Why a subfamily analysis was needed

The broad PF02627 alignment combines many divergent CMD-like lineages. A profile averaged across that entire set can lose a relationship retained in only one lineage. The 1,316 intact, explicitly CMD-annotated proteins of 80–119 residues were therefore clustered with MMseqs2 at 30% sequence identity and 70% bidirectional coverage. This produced 160 clusters. The 27 clusters containing at least five proteins were retained for profile analysis; the smaller clusters do not contain enough sequences to support a useful family HMM.

Each retained cluster was aligned independently with MAFFT and converted to an HH-suite profile. Both DehI halves were compared with every cluster, giving 54 pairwise tests. The complete ranking, including cluster sizes, effective sequence counts, model lengths and coverage, is available as the monomer-cluster result table, with cluster composition in the cluster manifest. The workflow is implemented in build_cmd_monomer_cluster_profiles.sh.

WarningInterpretation of the monomer signal

The corrected P-values show that these alignments are unlikely to be ordinary pairwise chance scores within this 54-test experiment. HH-suite probability also incorporates whether a match resembles true homologues rather than common background alignments, and the maximum here is 87.72%, below the conventional high-confidence range. The short 42–48-column N-terminal matches and modest profile diversity (Neff 2.9–3.4) are additional reasons to describe the result as suggestive evidence, not a family assignment.

Interpretation

Three features make this result supportive of internal homology:

  1. High profile probability. HHalign assigns a 99.54% probability to the N/C relationship.
  2. Domain-scale coverage. The 108 aligned columns cover 88% of the N model and 96% of the C model.
  3. Remote rather than trivial similarity. Direct identity is only 17%, yet conserved residue patterns remain detectable when information is combined across 69 family members.

Together with the near-identical structural folds reported for 3BJX, this strongly supports tandem duplication of an ancestral domain followed by divergence of the two copies within DehI.

The compact-CMD hexamer/AhpD trimer correspondence adds a useful, bounded observation: duplication and fusion of CMD-like cores has precedent within the broader structural group, and homologous cores can be redistributed between quaternary and tertiary organization. This comparison supplies plausibility for the evolutionary operation itself. It does not place AhpD on the path to DehI and is not used here to infer a stable DehI intermediate.

The external comparisons do not yet identify the ancestral domain definitively. However, separating intact CMD monomers into coherent subfamilies changes the picture: three related CMD profiles give probabilities of 74.52–87.72%, and CMD_monomer_015 matches both DehI halves. This makes a monomeric CMD relationship the strongest current external hypothesis. Because the probabilities remain below 95%, the most defensible conclusion is: strong evidence for internal DehI N/C homology and suggestive, but not yet conclusive, evidence connecting those repeats to a subset of monomeric CMD proteins. No comparable sequence-only support was recovered for AhpD.

If that CMD-family connection is validated, the evolutionary implication is more substantial than ordinary substrate or catalytic diversification. Duplication and fusion would have converted an ancestrally solvent-exposed functional surface into a buried interdomain cavity. The local fold could be retained while its biochemical context changed completely: solvent access, water organisation, electrostatics, substrate geometry, and leaving-group stabilisation would all become properties of the newly formed composite site. Subsequent asymmetric divergence could then concentrate direct hydrolytic catalysis in the C-terminal repeat while the N-terminal repeat increasingly provided enclosure and substrate positioning.

The conservative synthesis is therefore: DehI is strongly supported as an internally duplicated enzyme, and a subset of compact CMD-family proteins is the leading candidate for the ancestral fold. If this ancestry is confirmed, DehI would represent an unusual surface-to-cavity transition in which duplication and fusion created a new overall topology and a new catalytic microenvironment capable of different chemistry.

NoteWhat the result does not prove

HHalign measures compatibility between two profile models. It does not by itself establish the direction of ancestry, show that the ancestral protein performed modern γ-CMD chemistry, reconstruct the duplication-and-fusion intermediates, place AhpD on the DehI lineage, or demonstrate when dehalogenase activity first became selectively useful. Those questions require structural surface mapping, phylogenetic and genomic context, ancestral reconstruction, and biochemical testing.

Statistical qualification

Only one template HMM was searched (Searched_HMMs 1). Consequently, the reported E-value and P-value are identical (2.9 × 10^-23^); this is not an E-value corrected for a large profile database. The probability, score, and especially the broad alignment coverage remain directly informative for this pre-specified N-versus-C comparison.

Secondary structure was not included in the HHalign score (SS = 0, reported as missing dssp). This makes the profile match a sequence-derived result rather than one driven by the known structural similarity.

Robustness checks and next analyses

The following tests would strengthen the evolutionary interpretation:

  • Rebuild both profiles after removing 3BJX and repeat the comparison, ensuring that the structural reference is not driving the result.
  • Repeat profile construction with match-state thresholds of 30%, 50%, and 70% gaps and compare the aligned cores.
  • Search both DehI profiles against the same broad, curated HH-suite database and test whether their strongest domain-level matches overlap. A database search supplies the calibration and competing alternatives absent from these one-template HHalign runs.
  • Build structure-informed profiles for the lead CMD monomer subfamilies and compare representative structures with Foldseek/DALI. This is the most direct follow-up to the suggestive sequence-profile matches.
  • Superpose representative lead-cluster CMD structures or AlphaFold models on each DehI repeat, then quantify solvent-accessible surface area at homologous positions. The critical prediction is that a CMD-exposed surface maps to the buried DehI interdomain cavity.
  • Map aligned CMD/DehI positions onto the 3BJX active site and distinguish residues inherited from the ancestral fold from residues recruited after duplication for water activation, substrate binding, and halide handling.
  • Test the CMD_monomer_015 relationship against independently collected CMD and DehI sequence sets so that subfamily selection and validation use separate data.
  • Reconstruct plausible pre-duplication and early fused ancestral proteins. Structural modelling followed by biochemical testing could ask whether fusion alone creates a cavity or basal haloacid activity before extensive sequence optimisation.

## Summary

The configuration-inverting 2-haloacid dehalogenase DehI has two structurally
similar regions of approximately 130 amino acids. This analysis tests whether
the two regions retain detectable sequence homology consistent with an ancient
tandem duplication and fusion.

The N- and C-terminal regions were defined using the experimentally determined
DehI structure 3BJX, extracted from a 69-sequence family alignment, converted
into separate HH-suite profile hidden Markov models, and compared using
HHalign. The profiles align over nearly their entire lengths with **99.54%
HHalign probability**, an **E-value of 2.9 × 10^-23^**, and **17% direct
sequence identity**. The combination of low identity, strong profile-profile
support, and broad alignment coverage is consistent with remote homology
between the two DehI repeats.

Broad CMD-like and AhpD profiles produced no statistically convincing match to
either DehI half. The compact CMD proteins were therefore divided into
sequence-coherent monomer subfamilies before profile construction. This
revealed reproducible but sub-threshold signals: the strongest were
**DehI-N/CMD_monomer_017 at 87.72% probability** and
**DehI-N/CMD_monomer_015 at 85.88%**. CMD_monomer_015 also matched DehI-C at
**80.99% over 64 columns**. The first three corrected P-values remain below
0.001 after a conservative correction across 54 comparisons, but none reaches
the usual ≥95% HH-suite probability expected for a confident assignment.

If this relationship is confirmed, its importance extends beyond family
assignment. Compact CMD-family proteins present their functional surface to
bulk solvent, whereas DehI performs direct hydrolytic dehalogenation in a
buried active-site cavity formed within its duplicated architecture. Tandem
duplication and fusion could therefore have internalised an ancestral exposed
surface, creating a new physicochemical environment and enabling fundamentally
different catalytic chemistry. This is the central evolutionary hypothesis of
the report; the present sequence analysis makes it plausible but does not yet
establish the ancestry, direction, or historical order of those changes.

CMD/AhpD structures provide a relevant but deliberately separate precedent.
The compact CMD-family protein TTHA0727 forms a six-core homohexamer, whereas
trimeric AhpD packages six related cores as two core-like units within each
chain. This correspondence shows that duplication and fusion have redistributed
CMD-like cores between quaternary and tertiary structure in this broader group.
It does **not** make AhpD an ancestor of, or an intermediate toward, DehI.

::: {.callout-important title="Principal result"}
The N- and C-terminal profiles align across 108 columns: 88% of the N-terminal
model and 96% of the C-terminal model. This is a domain-scale match rather than
a short conserved motif.
:::

::: {.callout-warning title="External-family result"}
The current sequence-only profiles strongly support DehI internal duplication.
The monomer-subfamily analysis supplies a credible DehI-to-CMD lead, especially
CMD_monomer_015, but does **not yet establish** that relationship. Independent
profile-database and structure-based tests are needed before treating it as a
positive family assignment.
:::

::: {.callout-note title="Why the possible CMD relationship matters"}
The proposed transition is not simply a change in substrate specificity. It
would couple internal duplication and fusion to **active-site enclosure**:
an ancestrally solvent-exposed CMD-like surface would become part of a buried,
asymmetric reaction chamber in DehI. That change could alter water organisation,
electrostatics, residue pK~a~ values, substrate orientation, and halide-leaving
group stabilisation simultaneously.
:::

## Biological question

DehI from *Pseudomonas putida* PP3 is an unusual α-helical dehalogenase with a
deep Stevedore knot. Its monomer contains two regions with closely similar
structures but low pairwise sequence identity. The original structural study
described two approximately 130-residue domains with about 16% sequence
identity ([PDB 3BJX](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/3BJX);
[DOI: 10.1016/j.jmb.2008.02.035](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2008.02.035)).
A later topology analysis interpreted these regions as likely products of
tandem sequence duplication and described the proline-rich arc connecting them
([King *et al.*, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000731)).

The specific question addressed here is:

> Does a profile-profile comparison of the DehI N- and C-terminal families
> recover statistically strong, domain-scale sequence homology after the linker
> is excluded?

A second, exploratory question asks whether either DehI repeat retains a
detectable relationship to intact, compact carboxymuconolactone decarboxylase
(CMD)-like proteins or alkyl hydroperoxide reductase D (AhpD) proteins.

## Evolutionary hypothesis: from exposed surface to buried active site

### Structural precedent for duplication and fusion

The oligomeric architectures of compact CMD-family proteins and AhpD provide
precedent for duplication and fusion within the broader structural group. The
compact CMD-family member TTHA0727 ([PDB 2CWQ](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/2CWQ))
forms a D~3~-symmetric homohexamer built from six single-core polypeptide chains.
By contrast, *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* AhpD
([PDB 1KNC](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/1KNC)) is a homotrimer whose subunit
contains two structurally related core-like units. Structural comparison shows
that the AhpD trimer and compact CMD hexamer therefore contain an equivalent
six-core arrangement, with true two-fold relationships between separate CMD
chains replaced by intrachain pseudo-dyads in AhpD
([Ito *et al.*, 2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2242516/);
[Andreeva *et al.*, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkl746)).

This is evidence for an accessible *type of evolutionary operation*: related
CMD-like cores can be duplicated and fused, transferring structural repetition
from oligomeric assembly into a single polypeptide. It is not evidence that
AhpD lies on a CMD-to-DehI trajectory, nor that any particular oligomer was a
stable intermediate in DehI evolution. The DehI proposal below is an
independent duplication-and-fusion hypothesis whose distinctive consequence
would be active-site enclosure and formation of the knotted architecture.

### The structural transition

The possible CMD-family connection is biologically consequential because the
same local fold would operate in two very different physical settings. Compact
CMD-family proteins are small all-α units in which the relevant binding or
catalytic region remains accessible from bulk solvent. The structure of
TTHA0727 identified a solvent-accessible cleft as its putative small-molecule
binding site
([Ito *et al.*, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1110/ps.062148506)). In a structurally
related AhpD-like protein, the catalytic cysteines and adjacent surface cavity
remain externally accessible in both the monomer and oligomer
([Nunn *et al.*, 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3141380/)).

DehI presents the opposite biochemical context. Its two internally homologous
regions pack into a pseudo-dimer surrounding a buried active-site cavity. The
3BJX structure places the proposed water-activating Asp189 in this enclosed
site, supporting direct nucleophilic attack on either substrate enantiomer
([Schmidberger *et al.*, 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmb.2008.02.035)).

In this model, each duplicated unit need not adopt an entirely unrelated local
fold. The innovation occurs when fusion and subsequent packing change the
*context* of the conserved fold. A previously exposed surface becomes an
interdomain interface and reaction chamber, potentially changing several
catalytically important properties at once:

- solvent and water organisation around the reacting centre;
- local electrostatics and catalytic-residue pK~a~ values;
- substrate orientation and residence time;
- exclusion of non-productive bulk water; and
- stabilisation and release of the halide leaving group.

The two copies could then diverge asymmetrically. The C-terminal repeat could
specialise in water activation and catalytic chemistry, while the N-terminal
repeat increasingly contributes enclosure, substrate positioning, access
control, and structural support. This would convert duplicated structural
symmetry into a functionally asymmetric enzyme.

### Evolutionary importance and fitness

This would be an unusually substantial evolutionary transition: duplication
and fusion would connect a new overall topology, active-site burial, and new
chemistry rather than merely retuning an existing exposed pocket. The
CMD/AhpD oligomeric comparison establishes that duplication and fusion are
structurally precedented in the group, but it does not identify a viable
intermediate or show how dehalogenase activity emerged. Those questions remain
independent of the architectural precedent.

An immediate selective advantage is plausible but cannot be reconstructed from
the present sequences alone. Initial retention might also have been assisted by
ancestral catalytic promiscuity, improved stability or assembly, increased
dosage, or a period of effectively neutral persistence. Experimental studies
of other enzyme families show that weak ancestral side activities can become
the principal functions of post-duplication descendants
([Voordeckers *et al.*, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1001446)).

The ecological context supports selection for the *mature* activity without
dating the original innovation. PP3 was isolated under growth selection on
2,2-dichloropropionic acid, and its `dehI` family gene lies on the mobile DEH
element with its regulator, flanked by insertion sequences
([Webster *et al.*, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1128/mra.00089-25)). Horizontal
transfer could therefore have delivered an already functional DehI enzyme to
PP3, where haloacid utilisation supplied an immediate advantage, even if the
deeper duplication-and-fusion event occurred elsewhere.

## HH-suite profile construction

HH-suite was chosen because HHalign directly compares profile HMMs and is
designed to detect remote protein homology through alignment-to-alignment or
HMM-to-HMM comparison
([HH-suite documentation](https://github.com/soedinglab/hh-suite);
[Steinegger *et al.*, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12859-019-3019-7)).

Each split FASTA alignment was converted to A3M format. A column was designated
as a match state when fewer than 50% of sequences contained a gap (`-M 50`).
Columns not meeting this condition were represented as insert states. HHmake
then applied its standard sequence filtering and weighting before constructing
each HHM.

```bash
reformat.pl fas a3m results/dehi_N_terminal_msa.fasta \
  results/dehi_N_terminal.a3m -M 50
reformat.pl fas a3m results/dehi_C_terminal_msa.fasta \
  results/dehi_C_terminal.a3m -M 50

hhmake -i results/dehi_N_terminal.a3m \
  -o results/dehi_N_terminal.hhm -name DehI_N_terminal
hhmake -i results/dehi_C_terminal.a3m \
  -o results/dehi_C_terminal.hhm -name DehI_C_terminal
```

### CMD-like versus AhpD control

CMD-like and AhpD align at 97.04% probability over a 43-state core. This is
consistent with their shared broader classification and shows that the
external profiles retain a detectable common signal. It is not a fully
independent positive control, because the CMD-like PF02627 umbrella overlaps
conceptually with AhpD/CMD-like proteins even after annotation-based
separation.

## Monomer-focused CMD analysis

### Why a subfamily analysis was needed

The broad PF02627 alignment combines many divergent CMD-like lineages. A
profile averaged across that entire set can lose a relationship retained in
only one lineage. The 1,316 intact, explicitly CMD-annotated proteins of
80–119 residues were therefore clustered with MMseqs2 at **30% sequence
identity and 70% bidirectional coverage**. This produced 160 clusters. The 27
clusters containing at least five proteins were retained for profile analysis;
the smaller clusters do not contain enough sequences to support a useful
family HMM.

Each retained cluster was aligned independently with MAFFT and converted to an
HH-suite profile. Both DehI halves were compared with every cluster, giving 54

[monomer-cluster result table](results/dehi_vs_cmd_monomer_clusters.tsv), with

## Interpretation

Three features make this result supportive of internal homology:

1. **High profile probability.** HHalign assigns a 99.54% probability to the
   N/C relationship.
2. **Domain-scale coverage.** The 108 aligned columns cover 88% of the N model
   and 96% of the C model.
3. **Remote rather than trivial similarity.** Direct identity is only 17%, yet
   conserved residue patterns remain detectable when information is combined
   across 69 family members.

Together with the near-identical structural folds reported for 3BJX, this
strongly supports tandem duplication of an ancestral domain followed by
divergence of the two copies within DehI.

The compact-CMD hexamer/AhpD trimer correspondence adds a useful, bounded
observation: duplication and fusion of CMD-like cores has precedent within the
broader structural group, and homologous cores can be redistributed between
quaternary and tertiary organization. This comparison supplies plausibility
for the evolutionary operation itself. It does not place AhpD on the path to
DehI and is not used here to infer a stable DehI intermediate.

The external comparisons do not yet identify the ancestral domain definitively.
However, separating intact CMD monomers into coherent subfamilies changes the
picture: three related CMD profiles give probabilities of 74.52–87.72%, and
CMD_monomer_015 matches both DehI halves. This makes a monomeric CMD relationship
the strongest current external hypothesis. Because the probabilities remain
below 95%, the most defensible conclusion is: **strong evidence for internal
DehI N/C homology and suggestive, but not yet conclusive, evidence connecting
those repeats to a subset of monomeric CMD proteins.** No comparable
sequence-only support was recovered for AhpD.

If that CMD-family connection is validated, the evolutionary implication is
more substantial than ordinary substrate or catalytic diversification.
Duplication and fusion would have converted an ancestrally solvent-exposed
functional surface into a buried interdomain cavity. The local fold could be
retained while its biochemical context changed completely: solvent access,
water organisation, electrostatics, substrate geometry, and leaving-group
stabilisation would all become properties of the newly formed composite site.
Subsequent asymmetric divergence could then concentrate direct hydrolytic
catalysis in the C-terminal repeat while the N-terminal repeat increasingly
provided enclosure and substrate positioning.

The conservative synthesis is therefore: **DehI is strongly supported as an
internally duplicated enzyme, and a subset of compact CMD-family proteins is
the leading candidate for the ancestral fold. If this ancestry is confirmed,
DehI would represent an unusual surface-to-cavity transition in which
duplication and fusion created a new overall topology and a new catalytic
microenvironment capable of different chemistry.**

::: {.callout-note title="What the result does not prove"}
HHalign measures compatibility between two profile models. It does not by
itself establish the direction of ancestry, show that the ancestral protein
performed modern γ-CMD chemistry, reconstruct the duplication-and-fusion
intermediates, place AhpD on the DehI lineage, or demonstrate when
dehalogenase activity first became selectively useful. Those questions require
structural surface mapping, phylogenetic and genomic context, ancestral
reconstruction, and biochemical testing.
:::

### Statistical qualification

Only one template HMM was searched (`Searched_HMMs 1`). Consequently, the
reported E-value and P-value are identical (`2.9 × 10^-23^`); this is not an
E-value corrected for a large profile database. The probability, score, and
especially the broad alignment coverage remain directly informative for this
pre-specified N-versus-C comparison.

Secondary structure was not included in the HHalign score (`SS = 0`, reported
as `missing dssp`). This makes the profile match a sequence-derived result
rather than one driven by the known structural similarity.

## Robustness checks and next analyses

The following tests would strengthen the evolutionary interpretation:

- Rebuild both profiles after removing 3BJX and repeat the comparison, ensuring
  that the structural reference is not driving the result.
- Repeat profile construction with match-state thresholds of 30%, 50%, and 70%
  gaps and compare the aligned cores.
- Search both DehI profiles against the same broad, curated HH-suite database
  and test whether their strongest domain-level matches overlap. A database
  search supplies the calibration and competing alternatives absent from these
  one-template HHalign runs.
- Build structure-informed profiles for the lead CMD monomer subfamilies and
  compare representative structures with Foldseek/DALI. This is the most direct
  follow-up to the suggestive sequence-profile matches.
- Superpose representative lead-cluster CMD structures or AlphaFold models on
  each DehI repeat, then quantify solvent-accessible surface area at homologous
  positions. The critical prediction is that a CMD-exposed surface maps to the
  buried DehI interdomain cavity.
- Map aligned CMD/DehI positions onto the 3BJX active site and distinguish
  residues inherited from the ancestral fold from residues recruited after
  duplication for water activation, substrate binding, and halide handling.
- Test the CMD_monomer_015 relationship against independently collected CMD and
  DehI sequence sets so that subfamily selection and validation use separate
  data.
- Reconstruct plausible pre-duplication and early fused ancestral proteins.
  Structural modelling followed by biochemical testing could ask whether
  fusion alone creates a cavity or basal haloacid activity before extensive
  sequence optimisation.
